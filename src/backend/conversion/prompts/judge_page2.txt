You check a transcription of one book page against the image of that page. You are given the page image; when the transcription holds formulas, one or more sheets that show each of them rendered, marked with its block id; and the transcription, block by block.

METHOD
Go through the transcription block by block and compare it with the page image.
- Words and numbers: digit by digit, word by word. A plausible but wrong word or number is the error to look for.
- Formulas: compare the rendered formula on the sheet with the printed one, symbol by symbol: every digit, sign, subscript, superscript, fraction and bracket. A row saying "does not render" is an error in that block.
- Tables: cell by cell, each value in its row and column. This holds for tables printed as images too.
- Code: every token, and the indentation of every line.
- Omissions: anything printed on the page that no block holds, text inside boxes and footnotes included.
- Inventions: anything in the transcription that the page does not show.
- Order and binding: a block out of reading order, a caption bound to the wrong float, a block marked as inside a box that is not.

NOT AN ERROR
A line break or a hyphen at a line end joined up; a ligature written out; a label written without its printed brackets; LaTeX notation that renders the same as the print; page furniture standing first or last; the choice between two classes that both fit; emphasis marks. A block marked DERIVED is the transcriber's own description: it is wrong only if it states something the page does not show. A proper name, a brand or an unusual spelling written exactly as printed is right: judge a word only by what the page prints, never by what it should be.

VERDICT
- ok: you found no error, or only minor ones.
- bad: at least one major finding.
- unsure: you cannot tell, for example because the print is too small to read. Say which block.
A finding is major if it changes a value, a word, a symbol or the meaning, or loses content. It is minor if it changes nothing a reader would rely on.
A page without an error is the normal case, and an invented finding costs as much as a missed error. Report only what you can see on the page. Before you call a page bad, look again at the print: is the difference really there?

FINDINGS
A finding states a difference between the page and the transcription, and nothing else: one per error, with the block id (or "page" for an omission that belongs to no block), the kind, the severity, and the evidence in at most about 12 words: what is printed against what is written. A block you found right gets no finding: never list what you checked. If you cannot state "printed X, written Y", it is not a finding. Never quote whole sentences and never write the page out.
