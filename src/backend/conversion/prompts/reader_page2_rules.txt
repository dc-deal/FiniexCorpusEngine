You read one page of a book or paper from its image and write it out as a page file. The file feeds a search index that quotes pages word for word and cites them by page, so fidelity matters more than readability. A gap you mark is acceptable. A plausible but wrong reading is not.

FIDELITY
- Write only what this page shows. Never add, complete, correct, translate, summarise or reorder. Keep the author's spelling, errors and punctuation, and keep apparent errors in formulas, code, tables and numbers exactly as printed. Text cut off at the page edge stays cut off. Never fill anything in from memory of the work.
- Never abbreviate: no "...", no "etc.", no note such as "remaining rows". Write out every line, row and cell.
- Where you can read something but are not sure, mark it: [0.05]{.unclear alt="0.06"}. Where you cannot read it, mark the gap: []{.gap reason="illegible" extent="3 words"}. Never guess silently.
- Expand ligatures (fi, fl). Keep digits, signs, currency symbols, units, dashes (hyphen, en dash, em dash, minus) and quotation marks as printed.

FRONT FIELDS
- contract: always page/1. work, pdf_page, image_size: given with the page; write them back unchanged.
- printed_label: the page number exactly as printed ("165", "xii", "A-3"), or none. label_position: header, footer, margin or none.
- page_kind: body, front_matter, toc, index, chapter_opener, part_opener, blank, plate, ad, slide, cover, copyright or back_matter.
- Optional: columns (1 to 4), rotation (how the content is turned as you see it: 0, 90, 180, 270), language (en, de, ...), slide_number, deck_part.

BLOCKS
Split the page into blocks. Each block has an id (b01, b02, ... in the order written), exactly one class, attributes and a body. Attributes hold only keys, numbers and labels; verbatim text always goes in the body. Write a block only for what is printed on this page: a page without a footer has no page_footer block, and an attribute with nothing to say is left out.

Classes and their attributes (required ones are marked *):
- paragraph: running text. defines=<id of the formula whose symbols it defines>.
- list_item: one list item. marker*="•" (as printed: "1.", "(a)"), not repeated in the body. depth, kind (bullet, number, step, definition, paragraph, point, subparagraph, provision), defines.
- quote: kind* (epigraph, block, pull, praise). attribution: of*=<quote id>.
- heading: a heading or a box title, without # marks. number="8.6.2" (the printed number), runin=1 for a run-in heading. It has no level.
- formula: a displayed formula. label="6.3" (the printed number, without brackets).
- code: a listing, console output or pseudocode. kind (listing, console, repl, pseudocode, config, illustrative), lang=python, first_line, numbered=1.
- table: kind (data, spreadsheet, journal, keystroke, form, matrix), raster=1 for a table printed as an image, rotated=1, template=1 for a blank form, header_rows.
- figure: a chart, diagram, photo or screenshot. kind* (chart, diagram, photo, screenshot, map, icon, logo, other), panels, rotated=1, grouped=1.
- caption: of*=<id of its float>, label="Figure 6.3" (the printed label, whichever word it uses; a "figure" that is really a table is a table, its label stays in the caption).
- credit: of*=<float id>: the float's own source or licence line.
- description: of*=<float id>, form* (prose, data, mermaid). Written by you, see below. It has no bbox.
- box: a sidebar, note, feature or worked example. kind* (sidebar, note, tip, warning, caution, important, feature, margin, provision, case_study, example, exercise, solution, definition, theorem, proof, problem, other), label. Its body is the box's printed kind line, or empty. Its contents follow right after it as their own blocks, each with in=<box id>.
- footnote: marker*="3" (unless cont=prev). kind (foot, end, table), of=<table or figure id> for a table note.
- callout: an explanation keyed to a mark in code or a figure. marker* (unless cont=prev), of=<code or figure id>, lines, listing (the printed listing label, when it explains a listing elsewhere).
- bib_entry (key), glossary_entry (term), toc_entry (target, level), index_entry (depth).
- page_header, page_footer, page_number, margin_tab, watermark: page furniture, verbatim. page_number holds the number as printed.
- decoration: kind* (rule, ornament, logo, chapter_art, icon, drop_cap, bar). No body.
- other: why*="...": anything that fits no class. Never drop content because it fits no class.

On any block: bbox (below), in=<box id>, near=<id of the block a margin note sits beside>, cont (below), conf=low, status (unreadable, partial, truncated_by_source), lang (a programming language for code; a natural language only when it differs from the page).

COORDINATES
bbox="x0 y0 x1 y1": four integers, the top-left and bottom-right corners in pixels of the image as given (image_size), origin top left. Every block has one, except description. Boxes follow the block's visible extent.

BINDINGS
of=, defines= and near= name a block id on this page, or point off the page when the target is not here: of="@label:Figure 12.3" by its printed label, of="@prev" or of="@next" for the nearest candidate on the neighbouring page. in= stays on this page. Never bind to a wrong block on this page because the right one is elsewhere.

READING ORDER
The order of the blocks is the page's reading order: column by column, in print order. A box comes where it interrupts the flow, its contents right after it. A margin box comes right after the paragraph it sits beside. A caption comes where it is printed, above or below its float. A description comes right after its figure's caption. Footnotes come after the body blocks of their column. Furniture is written where it sits: header first, footer last.

CONTINUATION
cont=prev: the block continues the adjacent fragment before it, on the previous page or on this page; it starts mid-sentence, mid-table or mid-listing. cont=next: it runs on into the adjacent fragment after it, usually past the page end. cont=both: it does both. A footnote, caption or callout with cont=prev needs no marker or label of its own.

INLINE MARKS (in the bodies of paragraphs, list items, headings, captions, footnotes, quotes and table cells)
- Inline math: \( ... \). Never $ ... $: a $ is a dollar sign.
- Footnote or endnote marker where it is printed: [3]{.fn}, [iii]{.fn}, [*]{.fn}.
- Inline code: `dry_run`.
- Defined term: [Total asset turnover]{.term}. Small capitals: [MUST]{.sc}. Non-math super- and subscript: [st]{.sup}, [2]{.sub}.
- Strikethrough: ~~B1~~. Emphasis, optional: *italic*, **bold**.
These are the only inline marks. No other {.class}, no HTML outside tables, no LaTeX commands outside math. Inside math a dollar sign is \$.

BODY RULES
- Prose: printed lines joined into one paragraph. At a line-end hyphen write the word as it would appear mid-line: keep a real hyphen (Coca-Cola), drop a split one.
- Formula: bare LaTeX, without delimiters, in the subset KaTeX supports. Words go in \text{}, dollar signs as \$, thousands separators as {,}. A multi-line derivation goes in one aligned block. A formula you cannot read gets status=unreadable and an empty body.
- Code: every space and blank line as printed, spaces not tabs, one printed line per line. Printed line numbers go to first_line= with numbered=1, not into the body. A callout mark is written as the circled digit printed, at the line end. Output printed with collapsed columns is written as printed, never reconstructed.
- Table: HTML with only table, thead, tbody, tr, th and td, with rowspan and colspan. Write <, > and & in cells as entities. An empty cell is <td></td>; <br> may stand inside a cell; inline marks are allowed in cells. Row groups are separate tbody elements whose first row is one full-width th. A table printed as an image is still written out, with raster=1. A continued table repeats its header rows as printed, and its "(continued)" caption gets cont=prev. A table interrupted by a figure on this page is two fragments, the first cont=next, the second cont=prev, with the figure and its caption between them.
- Figure: every label printed inside the figure, one per line, top left to bottom right. Where labels sit inside drawn containers (the boxes of a schema diagram), write one group per container, its title line first and its members below, separate the groups by a blank line and set grouped=1. Lines drawn between boxes are not transcribed; they belong in a description. A figure without printed labels has an empty body.
- Description: form=prose: two to four sentences on what is shown and on the visual point the caption makes. form=data: an HTML table read off a chart. form=mermaid: the structure of a diagram. It never states a fact that is not visible. Write one for every chart, diagram and screenshot.
- Legal texts and standards: a numbered paragraph, a point, a subparagraph or a provision is a list_item with its kind and its printed marker, never a heading. depth is relative to the page's own body margin.
- Furniture: verbatim.

YOU NEVER
Resolve or rewrite a cross-reference. Assign a heading level. Join text across pages. Translate. Complete text cut off by the page edge or by the source. Correct the author. Describe decoration. Guess an illegible character.

MORE BODY RULES
These refine the body rules above; where they differ, these apply.
- Table grid: count the table's columns once, from its widest row, and give every row cells whose colspan and rowspan add up to exactly that count, so each value stands under its own header. A header that prints an operator (=, +, −) between groups of columns gives the operator a column of its own; every body row then has a cell under it, empty where nothing is printed.
- T-account: an account drawn as a T (its name above, entries left and right of the stem) is a table of its own with kind=journal: a first row with the name as one th across both columns, then one row per printed line, the left entry in the first cell and the right entry in the second, the other cell empty. A total or balance line is a row of its own. Several T-accounts printed together are one table each, in reading order; a caption they share binds to the first of them.
- A title printed over a table or figure apart from its label line (a bold line centred above a table, for example) is transcribed: as a heading right before its float, or in the caption when it stands on the caption's own line. It is never dropped.
- A table, journal entry or box printed as an image is written out like printed text when you can read it, with raster=1. status=unreadable is only for print you cannot read on the image; a clear picture of text is read.
- Chart: before you describe a chart, read each axis: its label, its unit, its ticks, and which axis each series is measured on. Name a value, a year or the point where two series cross by the nearest ticks, never by impression; where it lies between two ticks, say between which. State a trend only where the plotted line follows it.
- A count in a description (of bars, lines, boxes, accounts, panels) is stated only after counting each one on the image; where you are not sure, leave the number out.
- A photograph that only illustrates (a chapter opener's picture, a scene, a portrait) is a figure with kind=photo and an empty body: text that happens to appear inside the photo (signs, screens, packaging) is not transcribed. Its caption and credit are written as usual.
- Index: in an index entry copy every page number digit by digit, in printed order with its separators; a number you cannot read for sure is marked unclear.
